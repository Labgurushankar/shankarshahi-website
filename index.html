QC Troubleshooting Guide

Rule pattern → likely error type → practical first actions

1₂s warning

Likely: Early warning, possible random or systematic shift.
First actions: Check next QC result, review reagent/control handling, do not reject solely on 1₂s unless local policy says so.

1₃s

Likely: Significant random or systematic error.
First actions: Reject run, repeat QC after checking pipetting, reagent/control preparation, instrument flags and calibration status.

2₂s / 4₁s / 10x

Likely: Systematic error, shift or trend.
First actions: Review calibration, reagent lot, control lot, maintenance, temperature and recent changes; recalibrate when justified.

R₄s

Likely: Random error / imprecision.
First actions: Check pipetting, aspiration, bubbles, probe condition, mixing, sample volume and mechanical instability.

Sudden shift after new lot

Likely: Reagent/calibrator/control lot effect or recalibration issue.
First actions: Perform lot-to-lot verification, review calibration and target values, compare old vs new lot.

Gradual trend

Likely: Calibration drift, reagent deterioration, temperature or component aging.
First actions: Review maintenance logs, reagent stability, calibration trend, temperature and instrument performance.

These calculators are educational/practical support tools. Final QC acceptance, method verification, lot acceptance and critical-result handling must follow your laboratory's validated procedure, analyzer instructions, approved QC policy and applicable accreditation requirements.
